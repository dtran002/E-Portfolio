Learning reflections

This course gave me a strong foundation in the core principles of cybersecurity. I learned to think about security through the CIA triad:confidentiality, integrity, and availability — and how nearly every threat and defense maps back to one of those goals. We covered the main categories of attacks and threat actors, the role of cryptography in protecting data, authentication and access control, and how firewalls and network segmentation keep systems safe. The biggest idea I took away is defense in depth: the understanding that no single control is enough, and that real security comes from layering protections so that if one fails, another still holds. These concepts gave me a framework for thinking about security that I’ll build on throughout my degree and career.

How I grew

Throughout this course, I learned a lot of valuable information about cybersecurity principles and practices. I gained a better understanding of cybersecurity topics such as network security, different types of cyberattacks, risk management, and ways to protect systems and information. This course helped me build my knowledge and become more familiar with important security concepts. I also feel more confident and prepared as I continue studying for my Security+ certification and working toward my future career in cybersecurity.

A challenge I worked through

One thing I struggled with throughout this course was the cryptography sections. I wasn't very good at understanding some of the concepts and how the different encryption methods worked. Some of the information was confusing at first, and it took me longer to understand compared to other topics. I was able to get past this by going back through the textbook, reviewing the sections I struggled with, and taking extra time to study the material. As I continued reviewing, I started to understand the concepts better and became more confident.

Putting it to use

I plan to use what I learned both personally and professionally. Right away, I’ve started using longer, unique passwords and a password manager instead of reusing variations of the same one. Looking ahead, this foundation in hashing, salting, and cracking is directly relevant to a cybersecurity career — whether in penetration testing, defensive security, or just building systems that handle credentials responsibly. Even outside tech, understanding how accounts actually get compromised makes me a more careful user of everything from email to banking.
